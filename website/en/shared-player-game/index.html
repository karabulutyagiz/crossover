Two clubs. Find the shared footballer.

A shared-player game asks you to name a footballer whose career connects both clubs. You are recalling a real career, not choosing the best player or guessing a score.

How the shared-player game works

  1. Look at the two club clues. Think about players connected to both, rather than only their current squads.
  2. Type one valid name before the other player. There can be more than one accepted answer.
  3. Use the round result to learn a connection you missed, then move to the next pairing.

CrossOver Football brings this format to live 1v1 duels. You can challenge friends or practise against a bot. Club × Club is free on iOS; some other modes require the optional Social Pack. Read the full game rules before your first match.

Finding a connection without memorising every squad

Start with players who changed leagues, spent time on loan or returned to an earlier club. Separate “played for both clubs” from “transferred directly between them”: an intermediate club does not break the connection.

When you are unsure, use the shared-player finder. It looks up our selected club archive; it does not predict the next match or guarantee an answer is in the current game pool.

Is this football tic-tac-toe?

The idea of finding a shared footballer is similar, but the objective is different. In a duel you race to answer a pair. In football tic-tac-toe, each answer claims a cell and you need a line on the board. The guessing-format guide compares the alternatives.
